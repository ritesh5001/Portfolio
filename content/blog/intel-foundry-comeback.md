**Reports say Apple has reached a preliminary agreement for Intel Foundry to manufacture some of its chips, starting with low-end M-series processors on Intel's 18A-P process around 2027.** Neither company has announced a final contract, so treat product and date details as rumours, but even a preliminary deal would be the biggest validation of Intel's foundry business so far.

## What's reported

- **A preliminary Apple–Intel agreement,** first reported via The Wall Street Journal, potentially reducing Apple's dependence on TSMC.
- **Products and timing (rumoured):** analyst Ming-Chi Kuo says Apple plans to use 18A-P for its lowest-end M-series chips, with shipments around Q2 2027. Separate roadmap leaks put a future M-series chip on 18A-P and an iPhone chip on Intel's 14A by 2028.
- **Intel's process status:** 18A is reported to be in commercial production with yields above internal targets, and 14A showing promising early results.

## Why one foundry deal matters so much

Almost every advanced chip in the world, including Apple's and Nvidia's, is made by TSMC in Taiwan. That concentration is a risk for everyone: one supplier, one region, and limited capacity during an AI boom. A credible second source at the leading edge means:

- **More capacity** for AI chips, which eventually means cheaper AI inference.
- **Less geopolitical risk** in the supply chain that every device and server depends on.
- **Price competition** at the most advanced nodes, where TSMC has had little.
