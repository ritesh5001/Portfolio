**Sanand in Gujarat has become India's busiest semiconductor cluster: Micron, Kaynes Semicon and CG Power (with partners Renesas and Stars Microelectronics) are all running chip assembly and test plants there in 2026.** These are OSAT/ATMP facilities that package and test chips rather than fabricating the silicon, but they're the first large-scale chip manufacturing India has had.

## What's in production

- **Micron:** its assembly, test, marking and packaging plant opened in February 2026, with a target of around 6.3 million chips a day at full capacity.
- **Kaynes Semicon:** inaugurated on March 31, 2026, with roughly ₹3,307 crore invested. It makes intelligent power modules for automotive and industrial use, and its first output went to Alpha and Omega Semiconductor.
- **CG Power (CG Semi):** its first plant reportedly began commercial production in July 2026. It's a ₹7,600 crore project with about 300 million units a year of peak capacity, and a second phase is under construction.

Capacity figures differ slightly between sources, so treat them as approximate.

## What OSAT means

Making a chip has two big stages. **Fabrication** builds the circuits on a silicon wafer, which is what the upcoming Tata fab in Dholera will do. **Assembly and test (OSAT/ATMP)** cuts the wafer into chips, packages them, and tests them before they go into products. Packaging is increasingly where performance gains come from, especially for AI chips.
