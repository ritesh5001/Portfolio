**AI image and video generators (Google's Nano Banana image tools and Veo, OpenAI's Sora, Kuaishou's Kling and others) can now produce marketing-quality visuals in minutes.** For small brands that can't afford a shoot for every campaign, that's a big shift. But "can produce" isn't the same as "should use everywhere", and the trust and legal risks are real.

## Where AI visuals work well

- **Blog and social graphics:** illustrations and concept art, like the cover images on this blog, which were AI-generated.
- **Lifestyle backgrounds and mockups** for campaigns.
- **Storyboards and concepts** before a real shoot.
- **Short social video clips** and motion graphics.
- **Variations:** testing different visual directions quickly.

## Where to be careful

- **Product photos:** customers buy what they see. AI-altered product images that misrepresent colour, fabric or fit lead to returns and complaints. For e-commerce, real product photography is still essential.
- **People and faces:** don't generate images that imply real customers, models or endorsements.
- **Brand consistency:** generated visuals drift in style without careful prompting and review.
- **Rights and terms:** check each tool's licence for commercial use, and avoid prompts that copy other brands' artwork or trademarks.

## Label it

Platforms and regulators increasingly expect AI-generated media to be labelled, and tools are adopting watermarks like SynthID and C2PA content credentials. Being upfront protects trust.

## Speed matters too

AI images are often large files. Compress and resize them (WebP or AVIF) before uploading, or your beautiful new visuals will slow your site and hurt your [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-seo).
