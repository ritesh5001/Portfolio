**AI has made cyberattacks cheaper, faster and more convincing: phishing emails in perfect Hindi or English, cloned voices of business owners asking for urgent payments, and automated scanning that finds unpatched websites within hours.** Small businesses are prime targets because they have money, data and fewer defences. The good news is that the basics still stop most attacks.

## The threats that have changed

- **Phishing without the giveaways:** no spelling mistakes, personalised to your business, often referencing real suppliers or orders.
- **Voice and video impersonation:** a "call from the owner" or a "supplier" asking to change bank details. See my post on [deepfake detection](/blog/deepfake-detection-watermarking).
- **Faster exploitation:** automated tools find and exploit outdated WordPress plugins and exposed admin panels quickly.
- **Supply-chain attacks** through compromised software packages, like the [npm worm campaigns](/blog/npm-supply-chain-attacks) this year.
- **Attacks on your AI features:** prompt injection that tricks chatbots and agents into leaking data or taking actions.

## Defences that still work

1. **Verify payment changes by phone** on a known number. Always, no exceptions.
2. **Two-factor authentication everywhere**, ideally [passkeys](/blog/passkeys-replacing-passwords) or an authenticator app rather than SMS.
3. **Keep websites updated:** WordPress core, plugins and themes, and remove the ones you don't use.
4. **Backups you've actually tested restoring,** stored separately.
5. **Least-privilege access:** staff and plugins get only the access they need.
6. **Train your team** with real examples of AI-generated phishing.
7. **Limit what AI features can do,** and require human approval for sensitive actions.
