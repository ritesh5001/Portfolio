**AI meeting notetakers join your calls, transcribe everything, and produce summaries, action items and searchable records.** For client-facing work they're genuinely useful: nobody has to choose between listening and writing. But they also record people who may not have agreed to it, and they create a pile of sensitive data that has to be stored somewhere.

## Where they save real time

- **Client calls:** accurate requirements and decisions without frantic note-taking.
- **Action items:** who agreed to do what, by when, captured automatically.
- **Searchable history:** "what did the client say about delivery dates in March?"
- **Async updates:** team members who missed a meeting can read the summary in two minutes.

For client requirement calls especially, an accurate transcript is one of the simplest ways to avoid misunderstandings about scope.

## The risks

- **Consent:** recording people without clear consent is a legal and trust problem. In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act sets expectations for how personal data is handled.
- **Confidentiality:** transcripts of client, legal or HR conversations are sensitive, so check where they're stored and who can access them.
- **Accuracy:** summaries can drop nuance, misattribute statements, or miss sarcasm. Treat them as a draft, not a record of truth.
- **Bots joining uninvited:** some tools auto-join every meeting on someone's calendar, including ones they shouldn't.

## Rules I'd adopt

1. **Announce it** at the start of every call, and turn it off if anyone objects.
2. **Choose a tool with clear data controls**: storage region, retention and deletion.
3. **Don't auto-join** sensitive meetings like HR, legal or negotiations.
4. **Review the summary** before sharing it externally.
5. **Set retention:** delete raw recordings after a defined period.
