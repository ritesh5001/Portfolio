## What Indian customers expect from a website

**UPI first, cards second, COD always.** Most online payments in India now go through UPI, but a large share of shoppers still want cash on delivery for a first order from a brand they don't know. A checkout that only takes cards loses those buyers. I set up gateways like Razorpay with UPI, cards, net banking and wallets, and configure COD rules, including taking a small advance on COD orders to cut fake orders and returns.

**WhatsApp as the support desk.** Indian customers message before they buy. A WhatsApp button on product pages, order updates on WhatsApp, and a catalogue they can share with family are worth more than a contact form.

**Fast on a ₹12,000 phone over 4G.** If your site is only tested on a laptop with office Wi-Fi, it's slow for most of your customers. That means compressed images, fewer plugins and scripts, and pages that render on the server instead of waiting for JavaScript.

**Trust signals.** Clear return policies, GST details, a real address and phone number, and reviews. A first-time buyer in India decides whether you're legitimate within seconds.

## Which platform: WooCommerce, Shopify or custom?

- **WooCommerce (WordPress):** You own everything and pay no per-order platform fee. It's the right default for most Indian retail brands, and most of the stores I've built run on it, from silk sarees ([Samaraha](/projects/samaraha)) to farming supplies ([Krushi Doctor](/projects/krushidoctor)).
- **Shopify:** Less to maintain, with hosting and security handled for you. It suits brands that would rather pay a monthly fee than manage a server.
- **Custom software (Next.js, Node.js, PostgreSQL):** For business models a platform can't handle: multi-vendor marketplaces, B2B quoting, role-based dashboards and real-time messaging. [TatVivah Trends](/projects/tatvivahtrends), a wedding-wear marketplace, and [MariBiz](/projects/maribiz), a marine procurement platform with thousands of vendors, are both custom builds.

## Where AI actually helps an Indian business

AI is useful when it saves your team time or answers customers faster, not as a badge on the homepage. The practical uses I build:

- **Support assistants** that answer questions about products, shipping and returns from your own catalogue and policies, around the clock. I built [JARVIS AI](https://jarvisai.riteshgiri.dev), a chat platform with long-term memory using Google Gemini and Pinecone.
- **Multilingual content** in Hindi and regional languages, drafted with AI and checked by a human.
- **Smarter search** that understands "red silk saree for wedding under 5000" instead of needing exact keywords.
- **Planning and recommendation tools.** [Explore Fusion](https://explorefusion.online), my travel app, generates trip plans with the Groq AI SDK.
