**Consumer AI pricing has converged: ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro and Gemini's AI Pro all sit at around $20 a month, while the real competition has moved to free tiers and cheaper entry plans.** OpenAI sells a lower-priced Go plan and reportedly shows ads to some free and Go users in select markets. Google reportedly cut its entry AI Plus plan in mid-2026. Claude's free tier uses daily and rolling message caps. Prices and limits change often, so always check the provider's own pricing page.

## How the tiers break down

- **Free:** good for occasional use. Expect message caps, slower or smaller models at busy times, and sometimes ads.
- **Entry plans (below $10):** higher limits than free, aimed at price-sensitive markets like India.
- **Standard (~$20):** the frontier models, higher limits and features like file analysis and longer context.
- **Premium ($100–200+):** heavy users, long agent tasks and priority access.
- **API (pay per token):** what you use when building AI into your own product, priced per million input and output tokens.

## What a business should actually pay for

**For staff productivity:** standard plans for the people who use AI daily (writing, analysis, coding), and free tiers for occasional users. Team or business plans add admin controls and data protections. Check what each plan does with your data.

**For your product:** API pricing, not subscriptions. Your costs depend on model choice, context size and volume, not on a monthly fee. That's where [cost discipline for AI inference](/blog/finops-ai-inference-costs) matters.

## A simple rule

Pay for the plan that removes friction for the people who use AI most, measure what they get from it, and review quarterly. Prices are falling and changing faster than annual budgets assume.
