**ASML reported third-quarter 2026 net sales of €11.7 billion, a 57.5% gross margin and €5.0 billion of net income, and now expects full-year 2026 sales of €44–45 billion,** well above the €34–39 billion range it guided at the start of the year. For Q4 it expects €14.2–15.2 billion.

ASML is the Dutch company that makes the lithography machines every advanced chip factory depends on. Its numbers are one of the earliest signals of how much the chip industry is investing, and right now nearly all of that investment is driven by AI.

## Why a lithography company matters to a web developer

It's a fair question. I build websites and software, not chips. But the AI features your customers now expect (smart search, chat assistants, recommendations) run on GPUs made in factories that need ASML's machines. When ASML raises its outlook this sharply, it means chipmakers are adding capacity, and more capacity over the next few years tends to mean **cheaper, faster AI inference** for everyone building on top of it.

## What the numbers tell us

- **Demand is ahead of plan.** Moving from a €34–39B outlook to €44–45B within one year is a big revision. Chipmakers are ordering machines faster than they expected to in January.
- **Margins are strong.** A 57.5% gross margin suggests ASML is selling its most advanced (and most expensive) systems.
- **Q4 will be the biggest quarter.** The €14.2–15.2B guide implies a strong finish to the year.

ASML's release didn't headline a Q3 bookings figure, so I'm not quoting one here.

## What it means for businesses building with AI

1. **AI costs keep trending down over time.** More chip capacity tends to push down the price of each AI request. Features that are too expensive today, like an assistant that reads your whole catalogue, become routine.
2. **Don't over-invest in hardware now.** For most companies, renting AI through an API beats buying GPUs, especially while capacity is still ramping up.
3. **Build so you can switch.** New hardware brings new, cheaper models. Apps that can switch models easily benefit first.
