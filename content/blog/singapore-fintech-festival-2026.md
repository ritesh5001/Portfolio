**The Singapore FinTech Festival (SFF) 2026 runs November 18–20 at Singapore EXPO.** It's organised with the Monetary Authority of Singapore and the Global Finance & Technology Network, and is one of the world's largest fintech gatherings. This year's agenda centres on how financial institutions can use AI while managing AI-enabled scams, cyber threats and quantum-computing risks, alongside capital flows, talent and policy.

## Themes worth watching

- **AI in banking and payments:** from customer service and fraud detection to credit decisions.
- **AI-enabled scams:** deepfake voices, synthetic identities and highly targeted phishing.
- **Quantum risk:** the long-term threat to today's encryption, which I cover in [post-quantum cryptography](/blog/post-quantum-cryptography-migration).
- **Cross-border payments** and digital public infrastructure, a field where India's UPI is a frequently cited model.

## Why Indian fintech and e-commerce should pay attention

India runs one of the world's largest real-time payment systems, and Indian fintechs increasingly expand into Southeast Asia, often via Singapore. The questions on SFF's agenda (AI-driven fraud, scam prevention, trustworthy automation) are exactly the problems Indian payment apps, lenders and online stores are dealing with.

## Practical takeaways for any business taking payments online

1. **Use reputable payment gateways** with built-in fraud detection, like Razorpay and its peers.
2. **Add friction where risk is high:** step-up verification for large or unusual orders.
3. **Watch for scam patterns** like COD abuse, fake refunds and account takeovers.
4. **Secure logins** with OTP alternatives such as [passkeys](/blog/passkeys-replacing-passwords).
5. **Be transparent** with customers about how you protect their payments.
