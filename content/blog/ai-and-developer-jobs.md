**AI is reshaping the developer job market unevenly: entry-level hiring has fallen sharply while experienced developers remain in demand.** The Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index reports employment for developers aged 22 to 25 down nearly 20% since late 2022, while older developers at the same firms saw employment grow. Other reports show the same pattern of fewer junior openings and steady demand for senior engineers and applied AI skills, though their exact numbers vary.

## Why juniors are hit hardest

- **AI does much of the "starter" work** that juniors used to learn on: boilerplate, simple bug fixes, basic CRUD features.
- **Companies are cautious** about hiring and training when tools make existing teams more productive.
- **Expectations rose:** entry-level roles now often assume AI-assisted productivity on top of fundamentals.

## What's still in demand

- **People who can judge AI output:** reviewing, testing and catching subtle bugs and security issues.
- **System design and architecture.**
- **Product sense:** understanding users and business problems.
- **Applied AI engineering:** retrieval, evaluation, agents and cost control.
- **Ownership:** shipping and running things in production.

## Advice for developers in India

1. **Build real projects** with real users, not just tutorials. A deployed product with traffic beats a certificate.
2. **Use AI tools heavily,** and learn to verify what they produce.
3. **Learn fundamentals deeply:** data structures, databases, networking and security.
4. **Pick a domain** like e-commerce, fintech or healthcare, and understand its problems.
5. **Show your work publicly:** a portfolio, write-ups and open-source contributions.

## Advice for businesses

Don't stop hiring juniors entirely. Teams without a pipeline of learners struggle later. Pair juniors with AI tools *and* strong review, and they grow faster than ever.
