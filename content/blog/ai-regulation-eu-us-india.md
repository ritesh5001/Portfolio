**AI regulation is diverging by region. The EU has the most detailed law, the AI Act, but in 2026 it postponed the strictest "high-risk" obligations to December 2027 and August 2028 through its Digital Omnibus package, while transparency rules have applied since August 2, 2026.** The US relies mostly on existing laws, sector regulators and state rules. India has so far favoured guidelines and a sovereign AI programme over a single AI law, with a national AI framework announced as forthcoming. Always check current official sources, because these rules are moving quickly.

## The EU AI Act, in brief

- **Banned practices** already apply (for example, certain manipulative or social-scoring systems).
- **Transparency rules** apply from August 2, 2026: tell people when they're talking to a chatbot, and label deepfakes and AI-generated content.
- **High-risk systems** (AI used in hiring, credit, education and critical infrastructure) face the heaviest obligations, now deferred to December 2, 2027 for standalone systems and August 2, 2028 for AI embedded in regulated products.
- **It applies to non-EU businesses** whose AI is used in the EU.

## India's approach

India has emphasised innovation-friendly guidelines, data protection through the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, and public investment through the IndiaAI Mission, which I cover in [sovereign AI in India](/blog/sovereign-ai-india-data-centers). Sector regulators (finance, health, telecom) add their own expectations.

## What any business using AI should do now

1. **Disclose AI to users:** chatbots, AI-generated images and synthetic voices.
2. **Keep humans in the loop** for decisions about people, like hiring, credit and access.
3. **Document your AI features:** what model, what data, what purpose.
4. **Protect personal data,** in line with India's DPDP Act and the GDPR if you serve EU users.
5. **Label AI-generated media** and avoid impersonation.

Do these and you're well placed whichever way the rules settle.
