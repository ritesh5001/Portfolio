**Humanoid robots are moving from impressive demos into early factory and warehouse pilots, while home robots are getting gradually smarter thanks to the same AI models that power chatbots.** Tesla is planning dedicated chip production for its Optimus robot, as part of its [Terafab plans](/blog/terafab-spacex-tesla-chip-fab), and several other companies are testing humanoids in controlled industrial settings. A robot that tidies your home unsupervised is still years away for most people.

## What's real today

- **Industrial pilots:** humanoids and wheeled robots doing repetitive tasks like moving totes or loading machines in controlled environments.
- **Warehouse robots** (not humanoid) that are already everywhere: picking, sorting and transport.
- **Home robots** that vacuum, mop and mow, now with better object recognition and voice control.
- **Robot "brains":** AI models that understand instructions and plan actions, the biggest recent leap.

## What's still hard

- **Reliability:** a robot that succeeds 95% of the time fails constantly at scale.
- **Safety around people,** especially children and the elderly.
- **Dexterity:** handling soft, varied, real-world objects.
- **Cost:** humanoids remain far too expensive for most homes and small businesses.
- **Batteries:** hours, not days, of operation.

## What changes first

1. **Logistics and manufacturing,** where environments are controlled and labour shortages are real.
2. **Specialised service robots** for hospitals, hotels and inspection.
3. **Smarter home appliances** long before general-purpose home humanoids.
