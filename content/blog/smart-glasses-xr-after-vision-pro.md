**After Apple's Vision Pro showed what high-end mixed reality could do, but at a price and weight most people wouldn't accept, the industry's momentum has shifted to lightweight smart glasses: cameras, microphones and an AI assistant in frames that look like normal glasses.** Meta leads with its Ray-Ban and Oakley lines, Google is pushing Android XR with partners, and Apple's own glasses are reportedly [slipping to late 2027](/blog/apple-smart-glasses-rumors).

## Two paths for XR

- **Headsets** (Vision Pro, Quest): immersive and powerful, but heavy and used in sessions. Strong for training, design, gaming and specialised work.
- **Glasses:** all-day wearable, light, AI-first, often without a display at all, or with only a small one. The mass-market bet.

## What AI changes

The breakthrough isn't the hardware. It's that multimodal AI can now see what you see and answer questions about it: "What plant is this?", "Translate this menu", "Remind me where I parked". That makes glasses useful without full AR graphics.

## What it means for businesses

1. **Visual search grows:** customers pointing at a product and asking where to buy it. Your product data and images need to be findable.
2. **Local discovery through assistants:** accurate business profiles and structured data matter more.
3. **Hands-free work:** field service, warehouses and healthcare are early business use cases.
4. **Training and remote assistance** with headsets in manufacturing and maintenance.

## What not to do yet

Don't build a dedicated glasses app unless you're in a hands-free industry. For most businesses, the best preparation is a fast, structured website that AI assistants can read, which also helps you in Google and AI search today.
