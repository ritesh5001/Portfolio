**The competition for top AI researchers has reached extraordinary levels: reported packages above $10 million a year at leading labs, one-year retention bonuses, and high-profile researchers moving between Google DeepMind, OpenAI, Anthropic and others.** Reported moves in 2026 include Noam Shazeer leaving Google for OpenAI and AlphaFold Nobel laureate John Jumper moving to Anthropic. Analyses of hiring flows suggest the balance between labs is shifting quickly.

## What's driving it

- **A tiny pool of people** who have trained frontier models at scale.
- **Enormous stakes:** a single breakthrough can be worth billions.
- **Compute access:** researchers go where the GPUs are.
- **Mission and freedom:** experts say many researchers weigh research autonomy and a lab's direction as heavily as pay.

## What it means for everyone else

Almost no business competes for these researchers. But the talent war shapes the market you *do* hire in:

1. **Applied AI skills are in high demand.** Engineers who can build reliable AI features (retrieval, evaluation, agents, cost control) are valuable, and that's a learnable skill.
2. **You don't need researchers to use AI.** Frontier models are available by API. What most businesses need is solid product engineering around them.
3. **Small teams can punch above their weight.** AI tools let a few strong developers ship what used to need a larger team.

## Hiring advice for businesses

- Hire for **product engineering plus AI literacy**, not "AI researcher" titles.
- Look for people who measure results: evaluation sets, cost tracking, tests.
- Consider an experienced **development partner** for AI features, rather than competing for scarce full-time hires.
