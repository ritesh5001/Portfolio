**TSMC's 2nm process, called N2, is ramping up through 2026, and Apple is reported to have taken more than half of the initial capacity for its next-generation A20 iPhone chips.** MediaTek and Qualcomm are expected to follow with 2nm phone chips. Reports suggest TSMC aims to roughly double N2 output to about 100,000 wafers a month by the end of 2026, though these figures come from supply-chain reports rather than TSMC.

## What "2nm" actually means

Not much, literally: no feature on the chip measures 2 nanometres. Node names are marketing labels for a generation of manufacturing technology. What matters is what each generation delivers:

- **More transistors in the same space.**
- **Better performance at the same power, or the same performance at lower power.**
- **New transistor design:** N2 is TSMC's first node using gate-all-around (nanosheet) transistors, which control current better than previous designs.

## What improves for users

- **Battery life:** the most noticeable benefit in phones and laptops.
- **On-device AI:** more headroom to run models locally, privately and offline.
- **Thermals:** devices stay cooler under load.

Expect improvements, not miracles. Each generation brings meaningful but incremental gains, and early 2nm chips will be expensive.

## Why it matters for software

More capable phones make on-device AI features practical for more people, but in India most customers will use mid-range phones on older chips for years. Build for the device your customers actually have, and treat flagship performance as a bonus.
