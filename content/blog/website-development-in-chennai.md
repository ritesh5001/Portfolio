Chennai's economy runs on manufacturing: automotive and auto components, engineering, electronics, and the suppliers in industrial estates like Guindy and Ambattur that serve them. **For a manufacturer, the website isn't a shop. It's a sales engineer that works around the clock**, answering a buyer's technical questions and turning them into a quote request. Most industrial websites are built as brochures and fail at exactly that.

## What a manufacturer's website needs

**A product catalogue built for engineers.** Buyers search by specification: capacity, pressure, material, dimensions, standards. Each product needs its own page with a spec table, downloadable datasheets, drawings where you can share them, and applications. A single "Products" page with photos doesn't rank and doesn't convince.

**A request-for-quote flow, not just a contact form.** Let buyers add products and quantities to a quote request, attach drawings, and state delivery location and timeline. Your sales team gets a usable enquiry instead of "please call me".

**Credibility for procurement teams.** Certifications (ISO and others), plant and machinery photos, client logos (where you're allowed to show them), industries served and capacity. Procurement teams shortlist on these before anyone picks up the phone.

**Export readiness.** If you sell abroad, you need English that reads naturally, the right units, international contact details, and pages that load quickly outside India.

## When a manufacturer needs custom software

Past a certain size, the bottleneck moves from the website to the process behind it: quotes tracked in spreadsheets, dealer orders over email, no visibility into enquiry status. A small internal system often pays for itself quickly:

- **Quote management:** every RFQ from website, email and phone in one place, with status, owner and follow-up dates.
- **Dealer and distributor portals:** price lists, ordering and order tracking for your channel partners.
- **AI-assisted enquiry handling:** reading incoming RFQs, extracting products, quantities and specs, and drafting a first response for your team to check.

I build these with Next.js, Node.js and PostgreSQL, deployed so your team owns the code and the data.

## Questions manufacturers ask

### Can the site handle hundreds of products with specs?
Yes. Products, specs and datasheets are managed as structured data, so adding a product is a form rather than a design job, and every product gets its own searchable page.

### Should we show prices?
For standard parts, often yes, because it filters out mismatched enquiries. For configured or project equipment, a fast quote flow works better.

### Can enquiries go straight into our CRM?
Yes: Zoho CRM, HubSpot, Google Sheets, email, WhatsApp, or a custom quote system.
