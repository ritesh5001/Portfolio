**Generative engine optimisation (GEO) means making your website the source that AI search engines (ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews, Gemini and Copilot) quote and link to when they answer questions.** It builds on SEO rather than replacing it: AI answer engines still rely on crawlable, trustworthy, well-structured pages. But they reward some things more strongly.

## What AI answer engines look for

- **Direct answers:** a clear answer to the question in the first sentences of a section, not after three paragraphs of introduction.
- **Specific, verifiable facts:** numbers, dates, steps and named sources.
- **Clear structure:** headings that match real questions, plus lists and tables.
- **Crawlable content:** server-rendered HTML that AI crawlers can read without running JavaScript.
- **Authority and freshness:** real authors, real experience, updated content.

## A GEO checklist for your site

1. **Answer first.** Put the one-paragraph answer at the top of each page or section.
2. **Phrase headings as questions people ask:** "How much does a website cost in Lucknow?"
3. **Add structured data:** Organization, Person, Product, FAQ and Article schema.
4. **Show experience:** real projects, real numbers, named authors with credentials.
5. **Let AI crawlers in:** check `robots.txt` isn't blocking the crawlers you want, and consider an `llms.txt` file summarising your key pages.
6. **Keep pages fast and server-rendered.** I learned this when [my own React site served crawlers an empty page](/blog/react-seo-google-saw-empty-page).
7. **Earn mentions elsewhere,** because AI engines trust sources others cite.

## How GEO differs from classic SEO

Classic SEO competes for ten blue links. GEO competes to be *the* quoted source inside an answer, which means less traffic per query but higher-intent visitors, plus brand exposure even when nobody clicks.
