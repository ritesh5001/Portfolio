**Real-time AI video avatars (a realistic face and voice driven by an AI model that listens and responds live) have moved from novelty to usable product.** Businesses are using them for training videos, multilingual explainers, product walkthroughs and, increasingly, live customer conversations. The technology is impressive. The question is where it genuinely helps, and how to avoid eroding trust.

## Where avatars help

- **Training and onboarding:** update a video by editing a script instead of reshooting.
- **Multilingual content:** the same explainer in Hindi, English, Tamil and Marathi without hiring presenters for each.
- **Product demos and FAQs** on websites, especially for complex products.
- **Internal communication:** consistent updates for distributed teams.

## Where they don't, yet

- **High-trust conversations** like sales negotiations, complaints and healthcare, where people want a human.
- **Brand storytelling:** audiences can often tell, and a real founder on camera usually builds more trust.
- **Anything that pretends to be a real person** without saying so.

## The trust rules

1. **Always disclose** that it's an AI avatar. EU rules already require transparency for AI-generated content, and expectations elsewhere are heading the same way.
2. **Never clone a real person's face or voice** without explicit written consent.
3. **Label and watermark generated media**, using standards like C2PA content credentials where your tools support them.
4. **Give users a way to reach a human.**
5. **Protect yourself from impersonation:** the same technology powers deepfake scams, so verify unusual requests through a second channel.

## Using AI video on your website

Short avatar explainers can lift engagement on product and service pages, as long as the page still loads fast (lazy-load the video) and the key information is also in text, for search engines and accessibility.
