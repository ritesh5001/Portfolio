**In May 2026, Tata Electronics and ASML, the Dutch company whose lithography machines every advanced fab depends on, signed a strategic partnership for Tata's 300mm semiconductor fab in Dholera, Gujarat: India's first large front-end chip fab.** ASML will supply lithography tools and support the fab's ramp-up, and the agreement also covers workforce training, local supply-chain development and long-term R&D. It was signed in the presence of Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten.

Reports differ on the exact signing date (mid- or late May), so I've kept it to "May 2026".

## What the Dholera fab will make

- **A roughly $11 billion investment**, as reported.
- **Mature-node chips, reported at 28nm to 110nm**, for automotive, mobile, industrial and power applications. Not the cutting-edge chips inside AI accelerators, but the chips almost every product needs.
- **300mm (12-inch) wafers**, the modern industry standard.

## Why this is a big deal for India

1. **Front-end fabrication is the hardest part** of the chip supply chain. Until now, India's chip manufacturing has been packaging and testing, like the plants in [Sanand](/blog/india-osat-sanand-chip-cluster).
2. **Mature nodes matter.** Cars, appliances and industrial equipment run on these chips, and the 2021 shortages showed how fragile supply can be.
3. **Ecosystem effects:** suppliers, engineering jobs, training programmes and research partnerships grow around a fab.
