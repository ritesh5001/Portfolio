**Wearables are evolving from step counters into AI coaches that read your sleep, heart-rate variability, training load and recovery, and tell you in plain language what to do today.** Rings, watches and bands from most major brands now pair their sensors with AI assistants you can ask questions like "why was my sleep bad?" or "should I train hard today?".

## What AI adds to a wearable

- **Interpretation, not just numbers.** "Your recovery is low because of late meals and short sleep" beats a chart.
- **Personal baselines:** advice based on *your* normal, not population averages.
- **Adaptive plans:** workouts that adjust to your recovery and schedule.
- **Conversational answers** to questions about your own data.

## Where it falls short

- **Sensor accuracy limits everything.** Wrist and finger sensors estimate. They don't measure like medical equipment.
- **Generic advice dressed up as personal** is common in cheaper products.
- **Not medical advice.** Good products say so clearly and suggest a doctor when something looks off.

## The privacy trade-off

Health data is among the most sensitive personal data there is. Before buying, check:

- Where your data is stored and whether it's used to train models.
- Whether you can export and delete it.
- What's shared with insurers, employers or advertisers.

In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act sets expectations for how companies handle personal data, and health-tech businesses should design for it from the start.
