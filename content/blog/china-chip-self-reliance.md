**China is pushing hard to make its chip industry independent of foreign suppliers. Nikkei Asia reported in May 2026 an internal directive for more than 70% of the silicon wafers used by Chinese chipmakers to be sourced domestically by the end of 2026.** That's a reported internal goal, not a published policy. China missed a similar 70% target under Made in China 2025, though analysts see the narrower wafer goal as more achievable.

## What's happening

- **Wafer self-sufficiency:** silicon wafers are one area where China has made measurable progress, which makes the reported target more credible than past ones.
- **AI chips:** Huawei reportedly aims to ship about 1.6 million Ascend AI chip dies in 2026, but it's constrained at the 7-nanometre node and relies on stockpiled foreign high-bandwidth memory.
- **Domestic output:** one expert estimate puts Chinese domestic AI chip shipments at around 5 million units in 2026, up from about 4 million in 2025.
- **Money:** Bloomberg reported China is weighing incentives worth up to $70 billion for its chip industry.
- **Nvidia:** even with the US allowing some exports of Nvidia's H200 to China, Oxford Economics expects China's localisation push to continue, and Beijing has reportedly discouraged purchases of Nvidia's China-specific chips.

## Why it matters outside China

1. **Two AI hardware ecosystems are forming**, one around Nvidia and TSMC and one around Huawei and Chinese foundries. Models and tools increasingly get optimised for both.
2. **Supply chains are being split and duplicated**, which is part of why India is attracting fabs and packaging plants.
3. **Open-weight Chinese models** (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi) are often trained or served on domestic hardware, and they're competitive alternatives for developers everywhere.
