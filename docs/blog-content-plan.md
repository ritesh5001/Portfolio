# Blog content plan — riteshgiri.dev

Written 30 September 2026. Revisit every 3 months against Search Console data.

## What a blog can and can't do for this site

A blog does not make a site rank for every keyword. Broad terms like "web developer" or "website development company" are owned by agencies and directories with thousands of backlinks, and no amount of posting changes that in a year.

What a blog *can* do:

1. **Rank for specific, long-tail searches** where you have real experience and the competition is generic. For example, "woocommerce partial cod", "razorpay woocommerce not working" or "next.js rfq marketplace". Each one is small, but they add up, and the people searching them are close to hiring someone or solving a problem you can solve.
2. **Pass ranking value to the pages that make money.** Every post links to a service page and a case study. Posts that earn links lift `/hire-web-developer-in-lucknow` and `/services/*`.
3. **Make "Ritesh Giri" an entity search engines recognise.** Consistent, authored, dated posts tied to the same Person schema are how Google, ChatGPT and Perplexity learn who you are and what you're an authority on.

Expect the first posts to take **4–12 weeks** to get impressions on a young domain. Consistency matters more than volume: **2 posts a month, every month**, beats 10 in October and none after.

## How to write each post

**Before writing:** pick one primary keyword. Google it. Read the top 3 results and write down what they miss. That gap is your post.

**Structure**
- **Title:** ≤60 characters, keyword near the start, a specific promise. The build warns if it's too long.
- **Description:** ≤155 characters. It's the snippet under your title in Google.
- **First 2–3 sentences answer the question outright.** Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity quote the part of a page that answers directly, so don't warm up.
- **H2 headings phrased as the questions people search** ("How much does X cost?", "Why is Y slow?").
- **Length:** as long as the answer needs. Roughly 1,200–2,000 words for technical posts, 800–1,500 for client-facing ones. Don't pad.

**The part that actually makes it rank:** first-hand detail. Real screenshots from client work, real before/after numbers, real code, the mistake you made and how you fixed it. Generic "10 tips" content loses to sites with more authority; experience is the one advantage a solo developer has over them.

**Links in every post**
- Set `service:` in the frontmatter. The author box then links to that service page.
- Link to at least one relevant case study under `/projects/`.
- Link to 1–2 of your other posts where they genuinely help.
- When a new post publishes, go back and add a link *to* it from one older post.

**Using AI to draft** is fine. Google doesn't penalise AI-written text; it penalises unhelpful text. But a draft with no real details from your work won't rank, because it's the same as every other article on the topic. Always add what only you know.

## Publishing a post

1. Copy `content/blog/_template.md` to `content/blog/<keyword-slug>.md`.
2. Fill in the frontmatter and write the post.
3. Run `npm run dev` and open `http://localhost:3000/blog/<keyword-slug>` to preview. Drafts show there with a yellow banner. (If the dev server keeps restarting with an `EMFILE: too many open files` error, run `ulimit -n 65536` in that terminal first.)
4. Set `draft: false`, commit, and push to `main`. Vercel deploys it, and the sitemap, RSS feed, social image and blog index update on their own.
5. In Google Search Console, paste the post URL into **URL Inspection → Request indexing**.

## After publishing: get it seen

A post on a young domain doesn't get found just by existing. Within a day of each post:

- **LinkedIn:** a short post with the single most useful takeaway and the link.
- **dev.to and Hashnode** (developer posts): republish the full text with the **canonical URL set to your riteshgiri.dev post**. Both platforms have a canonical-URL field. You get their audience, and search engines credit your site as the original.
- **NextGen Fusion:** link to relevant posts from nextgenfusion.in. It's a related site you control, which makes it a legitimate first link.
- **Answer real questions** on Stack Overflow, Reddit (r/webdev, r/woocommerce, r/nextjs) or Quora when your post actually answers them. Answer in the reply itself, and link only as the "full write-up".
- **Client posts:** share in local Lucknow business groups, and as a Google Business Profile post once your profile exists.

## Measuring and improving

**Monthly, in Search Console → Performance → Pages,** filter to `/blog/`:

- **Queries at position 8–20:** you're close. Expand that post on the query's topic, add a section answering it directly, and link to it from another post.
- **High impressions, low click-through rate:** the title isn't compelling. Rewrite it (keep the keyword) and set `updated:`.
- **A query you rank for but didn't target:** that's a demand signal. Write a dedicated post on it.

**Every 6 months:** revisit your top 5 posts. Update facts and screenshots, then set `updated:`. Fresh, maintained posts hold their rankings better than ones left alone.
